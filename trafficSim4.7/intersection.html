How this generates intersections

Every road is a straight center axis with a width. Draw roads and snap their endpoints together to form a junction node. Selecting that node and clicking Make intersection runs the same construction used in road-network GIS pipelines:

1. Each road's two outer edges are offset ±width/2 from its center axis.
2. Roads at the node are sorted by angle. For each pair of angularly-adjacent roads, a circle of radius R is inscribed tangent to both facing edges — this is the rounded corner.
3. Where two roads are nearly opposite (a straight through-road, e.g. a T-junction's top), no corner exists — that boundary stays a flat cut instead of a curve.
4. All tangent points are connected in order — arcs where a corner was rounded, straight edges elsewhere — producing one convex polygon that works for T's, staggered crossings, 5-way junctions, or any angle.

Approach adapted from the offset-boundary + tangent-circle method described in “Automatic Generation of Large-scale 3D Road Networks based on GIS Data” (Wang et al., 2021), §3.2 / Fig. 3.
